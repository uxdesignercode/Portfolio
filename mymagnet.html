The Mandate

Ship first. Learn from real brokers later.

Before the app, Homesfy's partnerships team had already onboarded 2,000–3,000 brokers to Magnet. They got access to the same desktop CRM our sales team used. But brokers kept saying the same thing: "We don't sit at a desk."

Put the CRM in the broker's pocket.

No research phase, no competitor study. Get brokers onto the app first, then improve it from real use.

The brokers' reality (new small block, your strongest insight)

Most brokers work alone or with one partner. Their day is on the move: site visits, home presentations, builder–broker meets, and checking which new projects are coming up. A desktop CRM made sense for our office team, not for someone who's in a car or at a site all day.

The Problem

Brokers were flying blind

Homesfy had 10,000+ registered brokers selling real estate projects. The internal sales team had a full CRM. The brokers had nothing — no app, no visibility, no tools.

01

No mobile lead tracking

Brokers were adding leads by calling the ops team or messaging on WhatsApp. No app, no record, no status updates.

02

Fake leads flooding the system

Brokers could add any lead with no accountability. The sales team wasted time and budget chasing dummy numbers. No quality filter existed.

03

Zero campaign visibility

Brokers running digital campaigns through the platform had no idea where their money went — no PPC breakdown, no lead attribution, no ROI.

04

No project discovery tool

Brokers had to manually scroll through every project listing just to find commission slabs, inventory, or developer info — no filters, no search, no quick lookup.

Where it started

The first launch, built in two months to get the app into brokers' hands. Watching how they used it shaped every module above. Scroll inside a phone to see the full screen.

01 · How am I doing? What should I sell today?
V1 — first shipped

The home screen was a report. Brokers opened the app, saw their lead funnel, and closed it. Useful numbers, but no next step.

V2 — redesigned

The home screen is now a starting point. Search is on top. Hot and new projects show their commission % upfront. One tap on + adds a lead to that project. The funnel is still there, just lower down.

Design decision

Sell first, report second.
Brokers earn money by selling projects, not by checking numbers. So I moved project discovery to the top and the funnel below it. Now the first thing a broker sees answers the real question: "Which project should I push today, and how much will I earn?"

02 · Trust us Check it yourself
V1 — first shipped

Big numbers, no explanation. Brokers saw a total but couldn't see how it was made. And "Cancel Bookings" looked like a button that cancels a booking, which is scary on a money screen.

V2 — redesigned

The total now shows its maths: base brokerage + ladder incentive = total brokerage. Released money sits right below, in green. "Cancelled Bookings" is now clearly a list, not an action.

Design decision

On a money screen, trust comes from maths you can check.
Brokers don't trust a big number. They trust a number they can add up themselves. So I showed how the total is built, put "paid" right next to "earned," and fixed labels that could scare someone into thinking they'd lose a booking.

03 · Payments From empty boxes to a payslip for every booking
V1 — first shipped

Eight labels in three separate groups, often showing "--/--." Brokers could see the words "TDS" and "GST," but not how their brokerage turned into the money they actually got.

V3 — redesigned

One statement, top to bottom, like a salary slip: brokerage earned → deductions → GST and TDS → net payable. Released and pending sit side by side, and every unfamiliar term has an ⓘ.

Design decision

Brokers don't doubt the amount. They doubt the gap.
"I earned ₹3,60,000, why did I get less?" was the question behind most calls to accounts. So I turned the payment tab into a payslip: start with what you earned, show every deduction on its own line, then taxes, then the final amount. When the gap is explained line by line, there's nothing left to call about.

04 · Project list From one project per screen to six
V1 — first shipped

Big brochure-style cards. Every detail was there, but only about 1.5 projects fit on a screen. Comparing projects meant scrolling up and down again and again.

V2 — redesigned

A 2-column grid shows about 6 projects at once. Commission % sits right on the photo, with Hot/New tags, search on top, and quick actions to add a lead or run a campaign from the card.

Design decision

Brokers compare first, then open one.
A broker's first question is "which project pays me well, and where is it?" Price and BHK come later, once a client is interested. So I kept commission % and location on the card, moved price and BHK to the detail page, and fit 4× more projects on screen. Less detail per card, much faster to shortlist.

05 · Project Details From a spec sheet to a "should I sell this?" page
V1 — first shipped

All the facts were there, but in one long list. Slab, bookings and activity sat halfway down, the price was hidden inside the floor plans, and the icons had no labels.

V2 — redesigned

Everything a broker checks before pushing a project, in order: price, BHK, possession and slab first. Then why the project sells, how many bookings and activities it has, floor plans with prices, and videos of the project and the area.

Design decision

The broker reads this page to spot an opportunity.
A broker asks: How much will I earn? Is anyone buying here? Can I get more than one booking from this project? V2 answers all three on one page. Once they see the opportunity, they act from the bottom of the screen.

From here, the broker can Add Lead or Run Campaign. The three ways to earn are explained in the Add Lead deep dive below.

Module 01 · Home Dashboard

The first thing a broker sees every morning

The home screen needed to answer one question instantly: how are my leads doing? I designed it as a live dashboard — total bucket, today's leads, stage breakdown, and performance at a glance.

The problem

Brokers had no single view of their lead pipeline. They were calling the ops team just to know how many leads they had. Friction every single morning.

Design decision

Led with the bucket count as the hero number. Below it — today's leads, stage-wise breakdown (Calling / Answered / Follow Up / Site Visit), and conversion metrics. Dashboard-first, action-second.

Module 02 · Lead Listing

The CRM in your pocket

The lead listing mirrors the exact CRM workflow the internal sales team uses. Every stage — from first call to booking — is available on mobile. Brokers never have to call ops to update a lead status again.

The problem

The desktop CRM had a 15-stage lead lifecycle. None of it was accessible on mobile. Brokers were calling in updates or just not updating leads at all.

Design decision

Built the listing around the broker's mental model: ID, Client Name, Number, Project — the four things they actually check. Tap into detail for full history, call recordings, and stage updates.

Module 03 · Passbook

Know exactly what you've earned

Brokers were closing deals but had no real-time visibility into their payouts. The Passbook gives every broker a clear, deal-by-deal ledger — what's cleared, what's pending, and what's coming.

Module 04 · Digital Passbook

From blind spending to full ROI visibility

Brokers could run Meta, Google, SMS, and email campaigns through the platform. But once the money left their account, they had no idea what happened to it. The Digital Passbook was my answer.

Module 05 · Project Module

Every share becomes a personal lead funnel

This was my idea. I designed it and brought it to the PM — he loved it immediately. The insight: brokers share project details with clients over WhatsApp all the time. What if every shared link had the broker's number built in?

The problem

Before sharing any project, brokers had to manually scroll through every listing just to find the right one — no filters, no search, no quick lookup. And once the client got interested, they'd call Homesfy's main number — not the broker who sent the info. The broker lost the lead they generated themselves.

Design decision

Every project the broker shares carries their own profile. Tapping Share asks one question: send a link or a PDF. The link opens the project page with the broker's phone and WhatsApp built in. The PDF turns the whole project into a document with a broker card at the bottom. Either way, the client contacts the broker directly and the booking is credited to them, with no manual tagging.

How the share flow works
Broker opens project → Taps share (WhatsApp) → Link auto-tags broker's number → Client sees project + broker → Client calls broker directly → Booking attributed to broker
What the project listing shows
Commission slab per project · Developer info · Available inventory · Location · BHK configuration · Price range
The personalized share
Broker taps Share and picks a format.
Link: the project page with the broker's phone and WhatsApp added automatically.
PDF: the full project as a document, ending with a broker card: name, designation, years in real estate, specialty locations, phone and WhatsApp.
Why this matters

Before this module, brokers would share Homesfy's main project brochure and lose the lead to the company's own sales team. Now every share is their own funnel. This is the feature that turned Mymagnet from a lead tracker into a revenue engine for brokers.

Module 06 · Badge Profile

Your tier is your trust score

The badge system looks like a reward mechanic. It's actually a quality gate and a broker identity system. A Gold broker doesn't just get more leads — their entire profile looks different. It signals credibility to Homesfy's team, unlocks higher lead caps, and earns instant payouts.

The problem

Any broker could spam the platform with fake leads. The sales team was wasting budget chasing dummy numbers. There was no way to distinguish a serious broker from a bad actor.

Design decision

Designed tier progression as a trust system. Bronze starts at 10 leads a day. As brokers prove quality — real bookings, real clients — they move up. The badge on the profile is visible proof of that trust level.

Gold tier — badge profile

The badge on the profile communicates status instantly — a verified, trusted partner in the Homesfy network.

Bronze — 10 leads/day · Full RM support
Silver — 15 leads/day · Campaign leads
Gold — 20 leads/day · 0.25% bonus · Instant payout on first client payment
Post-launch iteration
Added profile tag system

After launch some brokers were still adding unqualified leads despite the tier cap. Added a tag system so team members could flag broker behavior — filtering out bad actors before they affected the pipeline.

Module 07 · Lead Add Process

Where commitment meets commission

Adding a lead in Mymagnet isn't just a form — it's where brokers choose their commission model. The add-lead flow is the moment of decision: how much support do you want from Homesfy, and what are you willing to give up in return? I designed this to make that trade-off completely transparent.

The problem

Three commission models, each with a different broker-effort trade-off. Brokers didn't understand why their split was what it was — or what they'd have to do differently to earn more.

Design decision

Built the lead-type selection into the add-lead flow itself. Brokers choose the lead type before submitting — they see the trade-off (commission vs support) at the exact moment of decision.

Module 08 · Search & Filters

Finding a project while the client is still on the call

Brokers look projects up under time pressure — a client asks about a locality, and the answer has to arrive in seconds. Search had to work from a half-remembered builder name, a locality, or nothing at all.

The problem

The only way to find a project was to scroll the full list. No search, no filters, and no way to narrow down by builder, location or configuration.

Design decision

Search opens with trending projects and recent locations, so an empty query is still useful. Typing narrows across project, builder and locality at once, and filters persist so a broker can work a shortlist across several calls.

Module 09 · Booking Timeline

Where a deal actually is, without asking anyone

A booking is not one event. Between agreement and payout sit registration, bank disbursement, invoice and release — each with its own delay, and each a reason for a broker to call the office.

The problem

Once a lead converted, the broker lost sight of it. Status updates came by phone call, and cancellations often reached the broker last.

Design decision

Modelled the booking as a vertical timeline with a state per step — done, in progress, blocked, cancelled. The broker can see which step is holding the payout and who owns it, which removed most status calls to the ops team.

Booking timeline

Step-by-step progress from booking to payout

Module 10 · Login & Onboarding

One number, no password

The broker's phone number is already their identity in the business — it is what clients call and what the CRM keys on. Making it the login removed the only credential anyone needed to remember.

The problem

The audience ranges from digitally fluent agencies to one-person brokerages who rarely install apps. Any friction at the first screen costs an activation.

Design decision

Phone plus OTP, with every field state designed — default, focus, filled, error, disabled — so a mistyped digit explains itself instead of failing silently. KYC comes later, once the broker has already seen value.

All Modules

Everything else that shipped

Run Campaign

Broker self-funds lead generation. Picks a project, sets a budget, chooses channel (Meta / Google / SMS / Email). Simple form — wireframed with PM. The passbook tracks results.

Home Loan Referral

Broker adds a client's name and number. If the home loan goes through, broker earns 2%. Minimal friction — maximum incentive to cross-sell.

Bulk Lead Upload

For brokers with 100+ leads. Download our Excel template (Name / Number / Project / Location), fill it, upload it. One shot instead of 100 manual entries.

Refer & Earn

Refer 1 broker who books → ₹5,000. Refer multiple → ₹10,000 cash + ₹10,000 in digital campaign leads. Growth loop + retention incentive in one mechanic.

Profile + KYC

Brokers must submit RERA number, GST number, company name, and government ID to unlock platform benefits. Hard block — no verification, no access. Keeps the broker network clean and legally compliant.

Error + Empty States

Designed three states: new user empty state (no data yet, show what to do), no internet screen, blank data screen (filters returned nothing). Brokers should never see a white screen and wonder if the app is broken.

What I Learned

Three things this project taught me

01

Design decisions are business decisions

The tier system, the badge caps, the personalized project sharing — these weren't just UI choices. Each one directly affected lead quality, broker revenue, and Homesfy's sales costs. Being a product designer on this project meant understanding the business deeply enough to make those calls — not just execute them.

02

Bring ideas, not just answers

The project sharing feature — broker's number auto-embedded in every share — came from me, not the brief. I designed it, showed the PM, and he shipped it. The best features I worked on weren't assigned to me. They came from understanding the user's daily behavior well enough to spot what was missing.

03

Post-launch is where the real design work starts

The passbook timeline, the profile tag system — both came after launch, from watching how brokers actually used the product. The first version answers the design brief. The second version answers the user. I learned to treat launch as chapter one, not the end.
