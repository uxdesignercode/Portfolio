Overview

Homesfy is one of India's leading real estate platforms. They help home buyers find verified properties across Mumbai, Thane, Pune, Navi Mumbai, Bangalore and Hyderabad.

I joined Homesfy as a Product Designer and took full ownership of redesigning the B2C portal. I worked on everything — from understanding the problem, doing research, creating wireframes, building the style guide, and delivering the final high-fidelity design. My goal was to make it easier for first-time home buyers to find the right property and trust the platform — and at the same time help Homesfy reduce external campaign costs and grow the business by promoting projects directly on the portal.

Discover — Research & Insights

Business Goals

  • Reduce dependency on Google and Meta campaigns by promoting focus projects directly on the portal — by region and micro-region
  • Increase user trust and return visits
  • Improve Google rating (was 3.5 — target was 4.5)

User Pain Points

  • Information overload — too many portals, inconsistent data, no clear way to compare
  • No advisor visibility — buyers don't know who will call them or how experienced they are
  • Discovery friction — hard to find relevant new launches by area, budget, and type quickly

Competitive Analysis

Before I started designing, I visited competitor websites — Housing.com, HomeBazaar, and Realtors. I took screenshots and checked how they show properties to home buyers. I used the websites myself to understand what is missing and what can be better.

Problem

Home buyers were overwhelmed by unstructured property information, unclear pricing, and heavy dependency on external digital campaigns. There was no in-platform way to promote builder projects, leading to high campaign spend and low trust. Home buyers had no visibility into who they'd be speaking to before making a call — just a generic Contact Us button.

Solution

Redesigned the Homesfy portal to provide transparent, structured real estate data with a powerful search and discovery experience. Introduced in-platform project promotion banners — reducing external campaign dependency while giving builders direct visibility to buyers. Introduced RM (Relationship Manager) profile cards showing years of experience, number of families helped, and area specialisation — turning a cold enquiry into a trusted conversation before the first call.

Results & Impact

The Google rating improved from 3.5 to 4.5 after introducing RM profile cards and transparent pricing. User satisfaction went up and support queries dropped.

What I Learned

1

In-platform promotion changes the business model

When focus projects are promoted directly on the portal by region and micro-region, external campaign dependency drops and the platform becomes more valuable to both builders and buyers.

2

Trust comes from knowing who will help you

The RM profile card was the most positively received feature during testing. Buyers felt more confident just knowing the name, face, and experience of the person who would call them.

3

Mobile-first is non-negotiable

Most home buyers in India browse on mobile first. Designing for mobile constraints first made the desktop experience cleaner too.

4

Data without context is noise

Showing '₹90L' means nothing without sqft, BHK, location, and possession date alongside it. The redesign was about surfacing context, not just data.
